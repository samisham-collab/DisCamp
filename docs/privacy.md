# DisCamp privacy policy

Effective {{ site.privacy_effective }}

DisCamp is a free map of dispersed campsites, run by {{ site.owner }} ("we"). This policy explains what DisCamp
collects, why, who else handles it, and the choices you have. Questions: **{{ site.support_email }}**.

**In short:** browsing DisCamp needs no account and sends nothing about you. If you sign in to add spots, photos or
cleanups, we keep your email address, your display name, what you post and the Embers you earn. Your location stays on your phone unless you use it to place a spot, post or clean up a cleanup, or get a route
preview. We don't show ads, track you across apps, or sell or share your information for advertising.

## Without an account

You can browse the map, read spots and set up your rig without signing in. Your rig, filters, map settings and an
offline copy of the spots are saved only on your phone. When the app loads spots, our host receives the request like
any website does (see "Service providers").

## If you sign in

You can sign in with Apple, Google, Microsoft or GitHub. We then receive and keep:

- **Your email address**, from the service you signed in with. It's never shown to other campers. If you use Sign in
  with Apple and choose "Hide My Email", we only get Apple's relay address.
- **An account ID**, a random identifier for your account.
- **Your display name.** It starts as the first name (or username) the sign-in service shares, and you can change it
  any time in **Account**. It shows on spots, photos, check-ins and cleanups you post.
- **When you agreed to the terms of use**, so we know you've seen them.

## What you post

- **Spots you add or edit** are public: anyone using DisCamp can see them, along with your display name and the date.
  A spot's location is wherever you put the pin.
- **Photos you add** to a spot are public, with your display name and the date. Before a photo leaves your phone,
  DisCamp shrinks it and saves it fresh, which strips the hidden details photos carry (location, camera and time,
  called EXIF). DisCamp asks for the camera only when you tap "Take a photo"; choosing from your library opens your
  phone's own picker, and only the photo you pick reaches the app.
- **Check-ins** ("Still good", or a change like a washed-out road) are public, with your display name, the date and
  any note you write.
- **Cleanups you post** are public: the before photo, what it is and how big, any note, your display name, the date,
  and where you stood when you took the photo (that's the cleanup's pin). Cleanup photos come from the camera only, and
  are shrunk and saved fresh like any DisCamp photo.
- **Cleanups you clean up**: you send an after photo, a photo of what you packed out, any note, and where you stood for
  the after photo, with how precise your phone said that was. Our moderators see all of it to check the cleanup. Once
  it's approved, both photos and your display name are public; where you stood for the after photo never is.
- **Embers**, DisCamp's points: a record of each one you earn and what earned it (a cleanup, a photo, a check-in).
  Only you see your record in the app. Embers have no cash value.
- **Reports you send** about a spot, a photo, a check-in, a cleanup or a name, with the reason and any note. Only we see them; the camper you report
  isn't told who reported them.
- **Campers you block.** Only you (and the app, to hide their spots and photos from you) see this list.

## Notifications

If you turn on notifications (DisCamp asks after you first send a cleanup, or in **Account > Notifications**), your
phone gives DisCamp a push address for this install of the app. We keep it with your account so we can tell you when a
cleanup you sent is approved or sent back, when someone cleans up one you posted, and, if you're a moderator, when a
cleanup is waiting for review. It isn't shown to anyone. Signing out on that phone removes it, deleting your account
removes all of yours, and you can turn notifications off any time in your phone's settings.

## Tips

Tips go through the App Store or Google Play. Apple or Google handle the payment; DisCamp never sees your card or
payment details, and we keep no record of who tipped. A tip doesn't change your account or unlock anything.

## Your location

DisCamp asks for permission to use your location only when you first tap "Show my location", "Sort by distance" or
"Use my location" (while adding a spot). Once you allow it, the app uses your location only while it's open, on your
phone, to show distances and center the map. It's sent to us only if you use it to place a spot, in which case it
becomes that spot's public location, or when you post a cleanup (it becomes the cleanup's public pin) or send one you
cleaned up (only moderators see it, to check you were there). If you open a route preview, your location and the
spot's are sent to our routing service to work out the drive, with your rig's routing settings (such as its length)
but nothing that identifies you; DisCamp doesn't keep them. DisCamp never tracks your location in the background.

## Crash reports

When the app crashes or hits an error, it may send a report to Sentry, our crash reporting service: what went wrong,
the app version, and your phone's model and operating system. We never attach your email address, display name or
account ID, and reports are scrubbed of sign-in codes and tokens before they leave your phone. They're used only to
fix bugs.

## How we use information

To run DisCamp: show spots, photos and check-ins and who added them, let you edit your own spots, keep your sign-in, review reports and
remove content or accounts that break the [terms of use](../terms/), prevent abuse, fix bugs, and answer you when you
contact us. We don't use your information for advertising, and we don't sell it or share it with anyone for their own
marketing.

## Service providers

These companies process information for us, only to provide their service:

- **Supabase** hosts our database, sign-in and photos. It keeps standard server logs (such as IP address and time of
  request) for security, for a limited time.
- **Apple, Google, Microsoft and GitHub**, when you choose to sign in with them. Their own privacy policies cover what
  they do.
- **Sentry**, for crash reports.
- **Expo** (650 Industries), whose push service delivers notifications to your phone, with Apple and Google's own
  notification services. They get the push address and the notification's text.
- **Apple and Google** handle tips you leave through the App Store or Google Play.
- **Resend**, which emails us each report so we can review it quickly.
- **Map providers.** The map uses Apple Maps on iPhone and Google Maps on Android, which load map images for the area
  you're viewing.
- **FOSSGIS e.V.** (Germany) runs the routing server behind route previews (Valhalla, with OpenStreetMap data). It gets
  your starting point and the spot's location for each route, and may keep standard server logs such as your IP
  address.

We may also share information if the law requires it, to protect campers' safety, or to respond to illegal activity,
such as reporting child sexual abuse material to the authorities.

## Keeping and deleting your information

We keep your account information until you delete your account. You can do that any time in the app: **Account >
Delete account**. Deleting your account removes your sign-in, email address, display name, block list and notification push addresses
right away. If you signed in with Apple on an iPhone, DisCamp also asks Apple to disconnect DisCamp from your Apple ID.
Spots, photos, check-ins and cleanups you added stay without your name, so other campers can keep using them, and your
Embers record is deleted; you can delete
spots and photos you added before deleting your account (except while one has reports waiting for review). Reports you sent are kept
without your account ID. Reports about your name are removed; reports about a spot or photo stay with it. A photo removed after review is
hidden right away, and we delete its file when we review it. Copies may
remain in our host's backups for up to 30 days before they're overwritten.

If we ban an account for breaking the [terms of use](../terms/), we keep a scrambled, one-way code (a hash) of its
email address and sign-in IDs, so the ban still applies if the account is deleted and made again. We keep the code,
not the address, and use it only to check new accounts against bans. We delete it if the ban is lifted.

If you can't use the app, email {{ site.support_email }} from the address you signed in with and we'll delete your
account. You can also ask us for a copy of your information or to correct it.

## Children

DisCamp accounts are for people 13 and older. Browsing doesn't collect anything about you, but if we learn that
someone under 13 made an account, we'll delete it. If you think that's happened, email us.

## Security

Your sign-in is kept in your phone's secure storage (the iPhone Keychain or Android Keystore). Connections to our
servers are encrypted. Our database only lets each camper change their own spots, photos and account. No system is perfect,
so we can't promise absolute security, but we'll tell you promptly if your information is affected by a breach.

## Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example in California or the European Union), you may have the right to access,
correct, delete or move your information, and to object to how it's used. Email {{ site.support_email }} and we'll
respond within 30 days. We don't sell personal information or share it for cross-context behavioral advertising.

## Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update it here and change the date at the top. If the change is significant, we'll tell
you in the app.
